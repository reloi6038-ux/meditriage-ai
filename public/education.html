! When to call emergency services

These are WHO general danger signs and major clinical red flags. If any apply, call your local emergency number immediately — do not wait for symptoms to improve on their own.

!

Severe difficulty breathing

Gasping, can’t speak in full sentences, blue lips, or struggling to breathe at rest.

!

Severe chest pain

Crushing pressure, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back, or chest pain with sweating, nausea, or shortness of breath.

!

Loss of consciousness

Fainting, unresponsiveness, or a seizure that doesn’t stop within 5 minutes.

!

Sudden neurological symptoms

Weakness or drooping on one side, slurred speech, sudden confusion, or sudden vision loss — think stroke.

!

Severe uncontrolled bleeding

Bleeding that won’t stop with pressure, vomiting blood, or blood in stool/urine.

!

Severe allergic reaction

Facial or throat swelling, hives with breathing difficulty, or after a known allergen exposure.

!

Signs of severe dehydration

Sunken eyes, skin tenting, no urine for >12 hours, or lethargy in a child or elderly person.

!

Stiff neck with fever

Neck stiffness plus fever and headache — think meningitis.

🩺 Common symptoms

Click each symptom for self-care guidance, when to see a doctor, and when to call emergency services. When in doubt, run a triage assessment.

🌡 Fever ▾

Adults: A temperature above 38°C (100.4°F).

Self-care

Mild fever under 39°C that lasts 1–2 days in an otherwise healthy adult: fluids, rest, and paracetamol/acetaminophen as directed. Monitor temperature every 4–6 hours.

See a doctor

Fever above 39.5°C, lasting more than 3 days, or accompanied by rash, stiff neck, confusion, or breathing difficulty.

Emergency

Fever with stiff neck, confusion, seizure, severe headache, or fever in an infant under 3 months.

💨 Cough & cold ▾

Most coughs and colds are viral and resolve in 7–10 days.

Self-care

Rest, fluids, honey for adults and children over 1 year, and humidified air. Antibiotics do not help viral colds.

See a doctor

Cough lasting more than 3 weeks, coughing blood, fever above 39°C, or worsening after initial improvement.

Emergency

Shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, blue lips, or severe difficulty speaking in sentences.

💧 Diarrhea & vomiting ▾

Most diarrhea is viral and resolves in 1–3 days. Hydration is the priority.

Self-care

Oral rehydration solution (ORS), small frequent sips, and continue eating if able. Avoid very sugary drinks.

See a doctor

Blood in stool, severe abdominal pain, signs of dehydration, or diarrhea lasting more than 48 hours in a child.

Emergency

Unable to keep any fluids down, no urine for >8 hours, confusion, or severe lethargy.

💓 Chest pain ▾

Chest pain always needs careful assessment. Cardiac causes are the most urgent to rule out.

See a doctor

Sharp pain that worsens with breathing (often muscular), acid reflux sensation, or pain only with movement.

Emergency

Crushing or pressure sensation, pain radiating to arm/jaw/back, pain with sweating or nausea, or chest pain with shortness of breath. Call emergency services immediately.

🧠 Severe headache ▾
Self-care

Tension-type headache: hydration, rest, over-the-counter pain relief, and stress management.

See a doctor

Frequent or worsening headaches, or a new headache in someone over 50.

Emergency

“Worst headache of my life,” headache with fever and stiff neck, headache with confusion, weakness, or vision changes, or headache after a head injury.

🫁 Shortness of breath ▾
See a doctor

Breathlessness with exercise or cold air, especially if you have asthma or COPD with a known plan.

Emergency

Breathlessness at rest, inability to speak a full sentence, blue lips, or breathlessness with chest pain or fainting. Call emergency services immediately.

5 Understanding triage levels

MediTriage AI returns one of five urgency levels. Here’s what each one means and what to do.

EMERGENCY

Seek emergency care immediately. Call your local emergency number. Do not drive yourself.

URGENT

See a clinician within a few hours (2–6). Don’t wait overnight for symptoms that are worsening.

SEMI-URGENT

Book a same-day clinical review if symptoms persist or worsen. Monitor for changes.

NON-URGENT

A routine appointment within 3–7 days is appropriate if you remain concerned.

SELF-CARE

Monitor at home. Seek care if symptoms worsen, don’t improve, or new symptoms appear.

👥 Special populations

Some groups need care sooner than the general population, even with mild symptoms.

Infants under 3 months

Any fever above 38°C in a baby under 3 months is a medical emergency. Call emergency services or go to the nearest pediatric emergency department.

Older adults (65+)

Elderly patients often present atypically. Confusion, falls, or a general “not feeling right” can be the only sign of serious infection or cardiac event.

Pregnant & postpartum

Any fever, severe abdominal pain, severe headache with vision changes, or reduced fetal movement should be assessed urgently. Postpartum fever within 6 weeks is a red flag.

Immunocompromised

On chemotherapy, post-transplant, or on immunosuppressants: any fever should be treated as urgent. Your threshold for seeking care is much lower.

Chronic conditions

Diabetes, heart disease, asthma, and COPD can decompensate quickly with seemingly mild symptoms. Follow your condition-specific action plan when symptoms start.

Children under 5

Children dehydrate faster. Watch for sunken eyes, no tears when crying, lethargy, and no urine for >6 hours. Use WHO/IMCI danger signs as your guide.

💧 Quick dehydration check

Especially important for children, elderly patients, and anyone with vomiting or diarrhea. If two or more signs are present, treat as urgent.

Sunken eyes
Skin doesn’t spring back quickly when pinched (skin tenting)
Dry mouth, no tears when crying
Capillary refill >3 seconds (press fingernail, count until pink returns)
Cold hands and feet
No urine for >8 hours (children) or >12 hours (adults)
Dizziness or fainting on standing
Confusion, drowsiness, or lethargy

What to do: Oral rehydration solution (ORS) in small frequent sips. If the person can’t keep fluids down, or has severe signs, seek care immediately.
