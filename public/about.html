About MediTriage AI

Built for the moment between symptoms and care.

A decision-support tool that helps community health workers, nurses, and patients decide how urgent a symptom really is — with explainable results, transparent reasoning, and clinical safety built into the architecture.

! The problem we’re solving

In low-resource clinics and community health posts, triage is often performed ad hoc — a conversation, a glance, a gut feeling. That works when the queue is short. It fails when the queue is long and one patient has a heart attack behind three others with cold symptoms.

Meanwhile, for patients who can’t easily reach a clinic, there’s no structured way to answer the most basic question: “Do I need to go now, or can this wait?”

What we’ve built instead

A structured intake workflow aligned with WHO/IMCI danger signs and emergency-severity principles, that returns a transparent 5-level urgency recommendation with an emergency flag, probability breakdown, action, timeframe, and watch-for signs.

Every result is explainable. Every danger sign is checked. Every emergency is caught before any statistical layer runs.

5 How the triage engine works

The engine is not a single model. It’s a layered pipeline where deterministic rules run first — because clinical safety cannot depend on a statistical layer that might be wrong.

1

Structured intake + validation

WHO danger signs, symptoms, vitals (temperature, HR, RR, SpO₂, BP, glucose, AVPU, pain), pregnancy status, chronic conditions, medications, allergies, and progression. Every field is validated on the client and again server-side with Joi.

2

Deterministic rule engine

Red flags, danger-sign combinations, and vital thresholds are evaluated first. A single red flag forces an EMERGENCY result before any statistical layer runs. This is the safety floor.

3

Bounded ML calibrator

A small logistic-regression model (trained on a reproducible synthetic benchmark) refines borderline cases. It is clamped by design: it cannot escalate a mild case to EMERGENCY, cannot downgrade an emergency, and cannot move a result by more than one rank.

4

Admin-managed clinical rules

Clinicians can add, edit, or disable rule overrides for their deployment — e.g. a regional policy that escalates specific fever presentations. Rules are prioritized, versioned, and audit-logged.

5

Deterministic AI summary

A non-diagnostic AI layer explains the result in plain language, flags missing information that would improve confidence, and suggests follow-up questions. It cannot override the triage result and does not diagnose disease.

✓ Safety guarantees

These are not aspirational goals — they are enforced by code and validated by tests.

Emergency rules always outrank every other rule
ML cannot downgrade an emergency result
ML cannot escalate mild cases to emergency
AI summary cannot override the triage result
Missing data reduces confidence — it never fabricates certainty
No names, addresses, or phone numbers stored by default
Every admin action recorded in an immutable audit log
Localizable emergency-resource directory — no hard-coded numbers

⚖ Transparency & limitations

We think honest disclosure of limitations is more useful than overclaiming clinical validation. Here’s what this tool is, and what it isn’t.

What we don’t claim

The triage engine has not been validated in a clinical trial. Its machine-learning calibrator was trained on a reproducible synthetic benchmark, not on real patient data, and is used only to refine borderline cases — never to override the deterministic rule engine.

  • Results are decision support, not diagnosis.
  • Weights and thresholds are based on published clinical guidance (WHO/IMCI, ESI), not on a validated proprietary dataset.
  • Local clinical policies may differ — the admin rule system exists precisely so clinicians can adjust thresholds to their context.
  • Failure modes include: under-triage of atypical presentations, over-triage when input is incomplete, and confidence scores that reflect the model’s internal calibration rather than true clinical certainty.

If you’re deploying this in a real clinical setting: commission a supervised pilot with a clinician-reviewed protocol, register it appropriately, and treat the app as a second-opinion aid only.
